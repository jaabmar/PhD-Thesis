\chapter{Introduction}
\label{chap:introduction}

Machine learning and statistical systems are increasingly deployed in healthcare, government, and software engineering. In these domains, predictive accuracy alone does not make an output safe to release or reliable to act upon. Practitioners face formal deployment risks across three stages of real-world machine learning: auditing observational causal studies for hidden confounding, releasing sensitive tabular datasets privately, and preventing language models from reproducing copyrighted training passages. This thesis develops statistically grounded, practical methods for these three problems in Chapters~\ref{chap:confounding-test}, \ref{chap:privpgd}, and~\ref{chap:cpfuse}, replacing brittle binary or geometry-blind heuristics with calibrated quantitative control under explicit structural assumptions.

\section{Deployment Risks Across the Data-to-Decision Pipeline}
\label{sec:intro-risks}

Standard benchmarks measure held-out predictive error, yet deployment imposes constraints that accuracy alone does not capture. In post-marketing drug surveillance, regulators pair randomized trials with large observational cohorts, where unobserved confounding can reverse estimated treatment effects \citep{vlahovic2011postmarketing,platt2018fda,klonoff2020new}. Agencies and hospitals share tabular records for public research while protecting personal data \citep{el2011case,abowd2018us,aktay2020google}. Large language models \citep{achiam2023gpt,team2023gemini} train on text and code containing copyrighted works, creating legal exposure when memorized passages resurface \citep{henderson2023foundation,karamolegkou2023copyright}. Naive safeguards in these settings often degrade utility sharply, scale poorly, or rely on brittle binary rules.

The first stage addressed in this thesis concerns the reliability of causal conclusions drawn from observational data (Chapter~\ref{chap:confounding-test}). Even when datasets are readily accessible, non-randomized treatment-effect estimates remain exposed to hidden confounding. Sensitivity analysis computes an observational critical value showing how strong hidden confounding would need to be to overturn a conclusion \citep{cornfield59,tan2006distributional,vanderweele2017sensitivity}. Because critical values use observational data alone, they do not measure how much confounding is actually present. Conversely, existing tests that audit observational studies against randomized trials test only the binary null of zero hidden confounding \citep{viele2014use,hussain2022falsification,yangelastic}, which can reject informative studies over small, harmless bias.

The second and third stages concern controlling what generative procedures reveal about their training data when releasing datasets or deploying language models. For tabular data release (Chapter~\ref{chap:privpgd}), differential privacy offers a formal individual-level guarantee \citep{dp,dwork2014algorithmic}, and marginal-based pipelines dominate empirical benchmarks \citep{tao2021benchmarking,hu2023sok,mst,aim}. Existing generators typically fit a graphical model to match discrete cell counts and sample from it, which becomes costly on large workloads, ignores feature geometry on continuous or ordered variables, and cannot easily enforce domain constraints. For language generation (Chapter~\ref{chap:cpfuse}), training-data memorization leads models to emit verbatim or near-verbatim copies of protected text and code \citep{carlini2021extracting,carlini2023quantifying}. Because copyright concerns substantial copying of expression rather than individual membership, it does not reduce directly to differential privacy \citep{elkin2023can,henderson2023foundation}. Practical deployment therefore needs inference-time protections that curb regurgitation without harming fluency or code correctness. Hidden confounding, record privacy, and copyright infringement are distinct mathematical problems, yet all three call for calibrated quantitative control built on explicit structural assumptions.

We organize the thesis around three chapter-level research questions and summarize our contributions to each.

\paragraph{RQ1: Quantifying hidden confounding with randomized trials (Chapter~\ref{chap:confounding-test}).}
\emph{How can randomized evidence be combined with observational sensitivity analysis to test confounding above a specified strength and obtain an asymptotically valid lower bound on true confounding?}
Observational critical values \citep{vanderweele2017sensitivity,jin2023sensitivity} do not measure actual confounding strength, while binary falsification tests \citep{hussain2022falsification,hussain2023falsification} cannot separate negligible residual bias from decision-altering confounding.

We cast marginal sensitivity analysis as hypothesis testing for candidate strengths \(\Gamma \geq 1\) (\Cref{def:confounding-msm}, \Cref{lem:confounding-testing-observation}). Under transportability of the conditional average treatment effect, internal validity, and support inclusion (Assumptions~\ref{asm:confounding-transportability}--\ref{asm:confounding-support-inclusion}), we build asymptotically valid level-\(\alpha\) tests comparing randomized target-effect estimates with observational sensitivity intervals for two target populations (Algorithm~\ref{alg:confounding-test}, \Cref{prop:confounding-test-validity}). Inverting these tests gives an asymptotically valid lower bound \(\widehat{\Gamma}_{\mathrm{LB}}^{\star}\) on the true confounding strength \(\Gamma^{\star}\) (\Cref{prop:confounding-lower-bound-validity}). Synthetic and semi-synthetic benchmarks confirm finite-sample validity and show how sample size and outcome-confounder correlation affect power and tightness (Figures~\ref{fig:confounding-synthetic-results} and~\ref{fig:confounding-hillstrom-results}). On the Women's Health Initiative \citep{anderson2003implementation}, comparing \(\widehat{\Gamma}_{\mathrm{LB}}\) with observational critical values \(\widehat{\Gamma}_{\mathrm{CT}}\) separates substantial confounding from negligible residual bias for coronary heart disease, whereas a binary rule flags both (Table~\ref{tab:confounding-whi}).

\paragraph{RQ2: Geometry-aware private tabular synthesis (Chapter~\ref{chap:privpgd}).}
\emph{How can marginal-based differentially private synthesis construct datasets directly from privatized marginals while scaling to large workloads, preserving feature geometry, and enforcing domain constraints?}
Graphical-model generators \citep{mckenna2019graphical,mst,aim} match discrete counts rather than ground-space geometry, while query-based methods \citep{aydore2021differentially,pmlr-v202-liu23ag} rely on predefined query workloads and scale poorly on large datasets.

We introduce Private Particle Gradient Descent (PrivPGD), which replaces distribution sampling with particle optimization in a compact Euclidean embedding space (Algorithm~\ref{alg:privpgd-algorithm}). PrivPGD projects noisy signed marginals to probability measures under the sliced \(1\)-Wasserstein distance and updates \(m\) particles via mini-batch gradient descent on the squared sliced \(2\)-Wasserstein loss. Sorting one-dimensional projections parallelizes cleanly on GPU, and differentiable domain constraints enter as additive differentially private penalties. Formal differential privacy follows from composition and post-processing (\Cref{lem:privpgd-privacy}). Across nine real-world datasets with at least \(50{,}000\) records each, generating \(100{,}000\) synthetic points from all two-way marginals at \(\epsilon = 2.5\) and \(\delta = 10^{-5}\), PrivPGD ranks first or second on downstream, covariance, and query metrics (Figure~\ref{fig:comp}). It improves geometry-aware sliced Wasserstein fidelity (Figure~\ref{fig:comp-sw-tv}) and protects targeted population-level statistics with little loss on unrelated tasks (Figure~\ref{fig:constraints-main}).

\paragraph{RQ3: Adaptive inference-time copyright protection (Chapter~\ref{chap:cpfuse}).}
\emph{How can inference-time model fusion prevent autoregressive language models from reproducing copyrighted passages, including quasi-verbatim spans and prefix extractions, while preserving text fluency and code correctness?}
Exact-match filters such as MemFree \citep{ippolito2023preventing} can introduce hallucinations or syntax errors, whereas token-wise fixed-weight fusion under the \(k\)-NAF framework \citep{vyas2023provable} still lets one base model dominate long continuations.

We introduce Copyright-Protecting Model Fusion (CP-Fuse) for two base models trained on disjoint copyrighted subsets under the \(k\)-NAF criterion (\Cref{def:cpfuse-knaf}). At each decoding step, CP-Fuse solves a local minimax Kullback--Leibler problem that tracks cumulative prefix likelihoods (Equation~\eqref{eq:cpfuse-opt-problem}, Algorithm~\ref{alg:cpfuse-algorithm}). Its Karush--Kuhn--Tucker solution yields a closed-form log-linear logit-fusion rule (\Cref{lem:cpfuse-kkt}) with a balancing property (\Cref{lem:cpfuse-balance}) that downweights whichever model dominates the prefix (Figure~\ref{fig:cpfuse-cumulative}). On overfitted 7B LLaMA 2 and StarCoder models, CP-Fuse reduces \(95\)th-percentile exact matches by more than a factor of \(25\) relative to the overfitted models and often by \(4\) to \(7\) times relative to \(\cpdelta\) (Table~\ref{tab:cpfuse-copyright}, Figure~\ref{fig:cpfuse-hist}). It also lowers quasi-verbatim and structural code copying while preserving pass@1 and fluency (Table~\ref{tab:cpfuse-utility}). Finally, CP-Fuse wraps training-time safeguards such as Goldfish loss \citep{hans2024like} (Table~\ref{tab:cpfuse-goldfish}) and early stopping (Appendix~\ref{app:cpfuse-early-stopped}), and resists prefix-prompt extractions (Figure~\ref{fig:cpfuse-prefix}).

\section{Thesis Organization and Publications}
\label{sec:intro-org}

Chapter~\ref{chap:background} introduces the shared mathematical foundations used across the thesis: observational causal inference with sensitivity analysis and test inversion, probability divergences and optimal transport, operational properties of differential privacy, and autoregressive decoding and copyright concepts. The three technical chapters then proceed sequentially across the deployment pipeline: Chapter~\ref{chap:confounding-test} develops the confounding-strength testing and lower-bound framework, Chapter~\ref{chap:privpgd} introduces PrivPGD for differentially private tabular synthesis, and Chapter~\ref{chap:cpfuse} presents CP-Fuse for copyright-protected language generation. Chapter~\ref{chap:conclusion} synthesizes the thesis findings, reviews limitations and structural boundary conditions, and outlines future work. Supplementary proofs, dataset details, ablations, and full tables appear in Appendices~\ref{chap:appendix-confounding-test}, \ref{chap:appendix-privpgd}, and~\ref{chap:appendix-cpfuse}, matching Chapters~\ref{chap:confounding-test}, \ref{chap:privpgd}, and~\ref{chap:cpfuse}.

Each technical chapter is largely self-contained and preserves notation standard in its literature. The front-matter Notation list therefore separates thesis-wide symbols from chapter-local symbols; letters such as \(T\), \(\mu\), \(\alpha\), \(\sigma\), \(\mathcal{M}\), and \(k\) carry distinct chapter-local meanings recalled where introduced.

Full bibliographic records and equal-contribution notes appear in the front-matter Publications list and at the opening of each technical chapter. In brief, Chapters~\ref{chap:confounding-test}, \ref{chap:privpgd}, and~\ref{chap:cpfuse} adapt three peer-reviewed conference papers \citep{de2023hidden,donhauser2024privacy,abad2025copyright}, edited into a uniform thesis presentation with supplementary material consolidated in Appendices~\ref{chap:appendix-confounding-test}--\ref{chap:appendix-cpfuse}.
