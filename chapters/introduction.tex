\chapter{Introduction}
\label{chap:introduction}

Machine learning and statistical methods are increasingly used in healthcare, government, and software engineering. In these domains, predictive accuracy alone does not make an output safe to release or reliable to act upon. This thesis studies three such settings: auditing observational studies with randomized trials (Chapter~\ref{chap:confounding-test}), private release of tabular data (Chapter~\ref{chap:privpgd}), and copyright-safe text and code generation (Chapter~\ref{chap:cpfuse}). For each setting, we develop a method and state the structural assumptions under which it applies.

\section{Three Deployment Settings}
\label{sec:intro-risks}

In post-marketing drug surveillance, regulatory and clinical practice increasingly use observational data alongside randomized trials \citep{vlahovic2011postmarketing,platt2018fda,klonoff2020new}. Unobserved confounding can make such observational evidence point in the opposite direction from randomized evidence, as in the Women's Health Initiative \citep{anderson2003implementation,vandenbroucke2009hrt}. Sharing sensitive tabular data in fields such as healthcare and government raises concerns about the disclosure of personal information \citep{el2011case}, and differential privacy is used by industry and government organizations to address them \citep{johnson2018towards,abowd2018us,aktay2020google}. Large language models \citep{achiam2023gpt,team2023gemini} can reproduce copyrighted material from their training data \citep{karamolegkou2023copyright}, a risk highlighted by several lawsuits \citep{henderson2023foundation}.

Chapter~\ref{chap:confounding-test} concerns causal conclusions drawn from observational data. Sensitivity analysis summarizes the robustness of such a conclusion by a critical value, the minimum strength that unobserved confounders would need to explain away the estimated effect \citep{cornfield59,tan2006distributional,vanderweele2017sensitivity,jin2023sensitivity}. Critical values are computed from observational data alone and can differ substantially from the true confounding strength. Tests that compare observational studies with randomized trials \citep{viele2014use,hussain2022falsification,hussain2023falsification,yangelastic} answer a binary question: whether hidden confounding can be detected. Residual confounding may be expected, however, and need not invalidate the practical conclusion of a study.

Chapters~\ref{chap:privpgd} and~\ref{chap:cpfuse} concern what generative methods reveal about their training data. In tabular data release (Chapter~\ref{chap:privpgd}), differential privacy gives a formal guarantee for each individual record \citep{dp,dwork2014algorithmic}, and marginal-based methods achieve state-of-the-art performance on many benchmarks \citep{tao2021benchmarking,hu2023sok,mst,aim}. A common approach fits a probabilistic graphical model to the privatized marginals and samples from it \citep{mckenna2019graphical}. This approach can become sensitive to the number of selected marginals, matches cell counts without using distances between feature values, and is less suited to enforcing domain-specific constraints. Query-based methods \citep{aydore2021differentially,pmlr-v202-liu23ag} instead construct a private dataset that answers a predefined set of queries.

In language generation (Chapter~\ref{chap:cpfuse}), memorization of training data leads models to reproduce protected text and code verbatim or nearly verbatim \citep{carlini2021extracting,carlini2023quantifying}. Differential privacy limits the influence of individual training records, whereas copyright protection concerns the reproduction of protected works, and it has been argued that the two goals differ \citep{elkin2023can}. Among inference-time protections, filters such as MemFree \citep{ippolito2023preventing} block exact matches but are less suited to quasi-verbatim copying and may produce hallucinations, while the fixed-weight fusion algorithm \(\cpdelta\) from the \(k\)-NAF framework \citep{vyas2023provable} can still produce long verbatim matches.

\paragraph{RQ1: Quantifying hidden confounding with randomized trials (Chapter~\ref{chap:confounding-test}).}
\emph{How can randomized evidence be combined with observational sensitivity analysis to test confounding above a specified strength and obtain an asymptotically valid lower bound on the true confounding strength?}

We cast marginal sensitivity analysis as hypothesis testing for candidate strengths \(\Gamma \geq 1\) (\Cref{def:confounding-msm}, \Cref{lem:confounding-testing-observation}). Under transportability of the conditional average treatment effect, internal validity, and support inclusion (Assumptions~\ref{asm:confounding-transportability}--\ref{asm:confounding-support-inclusion}), we build asymptotically valid level-\(\alpha\) tests that compare randomized target-effect estimates with observational sensitivity intervals for two target populations (Algorithm~\ref{alg:confounding-test}, \Cref{prop:confounding-test-validity}). Inverting these tests gives an asymptotically valid lower bound \(\widehat{\Gamma}_{\mathrm{LB}}^{\star}\) on the true confounding strength \(\Gamma^{\star}\) (\Cref{prop:confounding-lower-bound-validity}). Synthetic and semi-synthetic experiments confirm finite-sample validity and show how sample size and outcome-confounder correlation affect power and tightness (Figures~\ref{fig:confounding-synthetic-results} and~\ref{fig:confounding-hillstrom-results}). On the Women's Health Initiative \citep{anderson2003implementation}, comparing \(\widehat{\Gamma}_{\mathrm{LB}}\) with the observational critical value \(\widehat{\Gamma}_{\mathrm{CT}}\) separates strong confounding from negligible residual confounding for coronary heart disease, whereas a binary rule flags both cases (Table~\ref{tab:confounding-whi}).

\paragraph{RQ2: Geometry-aware private tabular synthesis (Chapter~\ref{chap:privpgd}).}
\emph{How can marginal-based differentially private synthesis construct datasets directly from privatized marginals while scaling to large workloads, preserving feature geometry, and enforcing domain constraints?}

We introduce Private Particle Gradient Descent (PrivPGD), which replaces sampling from a learned distribution with particle optimization in a compact Euclidean embedding space (Algorithm~\ref{alg:privpgd-algorithm}). PrivPGD projects noisy signed marginals to probability measures under the sliced \(1\)-Wasserstein distance and updates \(m\) particles by mini-batch gradient descent on the squared sliced \(2\)-Wasserstein loss. The loss and its gradient reduce to sorting one-dimensional projections, which parallelizes on GPU, and differentiable domain constraints enter as additive differentially private penalties. Differential privacy follows from composition and post-processing (\Cref{lem:privpgd-privacy}). Across nine real-world datasets with at least \(50{,}000\) records each, generating \(100{,}000\) synthetic points from all two-way marginals at \(\epsilon = 2.5\) and \(\delta = 10^{-5}\), PrivPGD ranks first or second in most metrics and datasets, with a few exceptions such as the covariance error on ACS Public Coverage (Figure~\ref{fig:comp}). It compares especially well under the geometry-aware sliced Wasserstein distance (Figure~\ref{fig:comp-sw-tv}) and protects targeted population-level statistics with little loss on unrelated tasks (Figure~\ref{fig:constraints-main}).

\paragraph{RQ3: Adaptive inference-time copyright protection (Chapter~\ref{chap:cpfuse}).}
\emph{How can inference-time model fusion prevent autoregressive language models from reproducing copyrighted passages, including quasi-verbatim spans and prefix extractions, while preserving text fluency and code correctness?}

We introduce Copyright-Protecting Model Fusion (CP-Fuse), which combines two base models trained on data containing disjoint sets of copyrighted material, building on the \(k\)-NAF criterion (\Cref{def:cpfuse-knaf}). At each decoding step, CP-Fuse solves a local minimax Kullback--Leibler problem that tracks the prefix likelihoods under both models (Equation~\eqref{eq:cpfuse-opt-problem}, Algorithm~\ref{alg:cpfuse-algorithm}). The Karush--Kuhn--Tucker conditions give the solution a log-linear closed form (\Cref{lem:cpfuse-kkt}), whose weights are chosen by a grid search in practice. A balancing property (\Cref{lem:cpfuse-balance}) downweights whichever model dominates the prefix (Figure~\ref{fig:cpfuse-cumulative}). On overfitted 7B LLaMA 2 and StarCoder models, CP-Fuse reduces \(95\)th-percentile exact matches by more than \(20\times\) on the three main datasets relative to the overfitted models, and by up to about \(6\times\) relative to \(\cpdelta\) (Table~\ref{tab:cpfuse-copyright}, Figure~\ref{fig:cpfuse-hist}). It also lowers quasi-verbatim and structural code copying, while pass@1 and fluency stay close to those of the overfitted models (Table~\ref{tab:cpfuse-utility}). CP-Fuse can be applied on top of training-time safeguards such as Goldfish loss \citep{hans2024like} (Table~\ref{tab:cpfuse-goldfish}) and early stopping (Appendix~\ref{app:cpfuse-early-stopped}), and its protection remains largely intact under prefix-prompt extraction (Figure~\ref{fig:cpfuse-prefix}).

\section{Thesis Organization and Publications}
\label{sec:intro-org}

Chapter~\ref{chap:background} introduces the background used across the thesis: observational causal inference with sensitivity analysis and test inversion, probability divergences and optimal transport, differential privacy, and autoregressive decoding and copyright. Chapter~\ref{chap:confounding-test} develops the confounding-strength tests and lower bound, Chapter~\ref{chap:privpgd} introduces PrivPGD for differentially private tabular synthesis, and Chapter~\ref{chap:cpfuse} presents CP-Fuse for copyright-protected language generation. Chapter~\ref{chap:conclusion} summarizes the findings, discusses limitations, and outlines future work. Proofs, dataset details, ablations, and full tables appear in Appendices~\ref{chap:appendix-confounding-test}, \ref{chap:appendix-privpgd}, and~\ref{chap:appendix-cpfuse}, which accompany Chapters~\ref{chap:confounding-test}, \ref{chap:privpgd}, and~\ref{chap:cpfuse}.

Each technical chapter is largely self-contained and keeps the notation standard in its literature, and the front-matter Notation list separates thesis-wide symbols from chapter-local ones.

Chapters~\ref{chap:confounding-test}, \ref{chap:privpgd}, and~\ref{chap:cpfuse} adapt three peer-reviewed conference papers \citep{de2023hidden,donhauser2024privacy,abad2025copyright}. Full bibliographic records and contribution notes appear in the front-matter Publications list and at the opening of each technical chapter.
